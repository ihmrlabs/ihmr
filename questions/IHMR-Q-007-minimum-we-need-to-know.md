# What is the least we need to know about a person?

> **AI disclosure:** AI-assisted research · Awaiting sign-off

The instinct with any system like this is to collect everything. We think that instinct is
wrong, and we would like to know how much less would actually do.

## Why it matters

More data is not free. It is more risk if it leaks, more burden to collect, more to keep
current, and more that can be wrong. A system holding everything about everyone is also a very
different thing politically from one holding the minimum it needs.

Our working principle: **keep the minimum sufficient model of a person needed to make good
decisions.** We do not yet know what that is.

## The questions inside the question

- What genuinely changes a decision, and what merely feels relevant?
- How much can be inferred rather than collected?
- What has to be current, and what is fine being years old?
- How does this work for someone with almost no recorded history, which is a great many people?
- What does the system do under partial or intermittent connectivity?

## A distinction we keep returning to

A health record answers *what happened*. Something that maintains health has to also answer
*what should happen next*. Those need different information, and the second may need
surprisingly little.

There is a real risk of this project drifting into simply centralising more records. We would
consider that a failure even if the records were excellent.

## What would count as an answer

A defensible minimum set, with reasoning for each element and an honest account of what is lost
by excluding things. Ideally tested in simulation against what fuller data would have concluded.

## Help us with this

Clinicians: what do you actually need to know before deciding what someone needs next? Privacy
researchers: what would you refuse to hold?
