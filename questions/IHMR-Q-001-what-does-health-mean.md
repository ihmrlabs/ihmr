# What does health actually mean, in operational terms?


If a system is going to maintain something, it has to know what it is maintaining. This is the
first question, and everything else inherits its answer.

## Why it matters

"Absence of diagnosed disease" is clearly not enough. A 25 year old with severe metabolic risk,
poor sleep and rapidly worsening markers is not healthy in any useful sense, even with an empty
medical record. An 80 year old with well controlled chronic disease who is mobile, independent,
cognitively sharp and connected to people they love may be doing very well indeed.

The risk is precise and worth stating: **if we define health badly, we can build a very
efficient system that optimises the wrong thing.** That failure would be invisible from inside
the system, and expensive to undo.

## Where our thinking currently sits

A working definition we have been using, which we do not think is final:

> Health is a person's ability to live a long life with high physical, mental and functional
> capacity, while minimising preventable disease, disability and suffering.

Candidate things to optimise for include healthy life expectancy, functional capacity,
quality-adjusted life years, preventable mortality, avoidable disability, disease control,
independence, mental wellbeing, and the person's own stated goals.

## What would count as an answer

- A definition that can be measured without collapsing into whatever happens to be measurable
- Clarity on how much an individual's own preferences shape the objective
- A defensible account of how mental health and social determinants sit inside it
- Something a clinician, a health economist and a person receiving care would all recognise

## What we are wary of

**Measurability quietly overwriting the definition.** Blood pressure, HbA1c, screening
completion and vaccination status are all easy to verify. Functional capacity, social
connection and preventable suffering are not. There is a real risk that whatever the system can
close the loop on becomes what it optimises, regardless of what this document says.

We think defining health before deciding what to measure is a genuine protection against that.
We do not think it is a complete one.
