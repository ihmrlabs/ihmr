# Can a city have a health state of its own?


People do not live in the abstract. They live in neighbourhoods, breathe particular air, walk
or fail to walk particular streets, and buy food from whatever is nearby.

## Why it matters

A system that maintains individual health while ignoring the environment continuously shaping
it will eventually hit a ceiling. You can treat the same condition over and over without ever
touching what is causing it.

So there may be two layers here rather than one: maintain the person, and improve the place the
person is being maintained in.

## The questions inside the question

- What actually makes a city healthy, in measurable terms?
- Which health variables are genuinely controllable at municipal level?
- Can a place have its own outstanding public health needs, distinct from any individual's?
- How do air quality, heat, walkability, food access, water, sanitation, housing, commute time,
  noise, safety and loneliness feed into individual health in ways that could be acted on?
- Can neighbourhood level risk be identified without compromising anyone's privacy?
- What would it mean for a city to become measurably healthier over ten years?

## Why this might matter more than it first appears

City level work needs much less of what is hardest to get. No individual consent architecture,
no clinical governance for personal recommendations, no identity resolution. It can produce
visible results faster.

There is a real argument that a city is the right first proving ground, and that something like
a "Healthier Chennai" might be a better first real artefact than any person level pilot.
