# Who actually does the work?

> **AI disclosure:** AI-assisted research · Awaiting sign-off

Knowing what should happen is not the same as making it happen. Identifying a need and then
having nobody to act on it produces a very well organised list that helps nobody.

## Why it matters

If a system like this worked, it would surface an enormous number of outstanding needs at once.
The binding constraint would immediately stop being information and start being people: clinician
hours, health centre throughput, diagnostic capacity, medicine supply.

That changes the design. It makes deciding what **not** to surface as important as deciding what
to surface, which is a rationing question with real ethical weight that we should not pretend is
merely technical.

## Where our thinking currently sits

India's community health workforce is roughly a million ASHAs, plus ANMs, Anganwadi workers and
an extensive primary care network. That is among the largest in the world.

Rwanda has shown that community health workers plus good coordination can produce results at
national scale. We think the interesting possibility is not that technology replaces these
workers, but that it makes each of them considerably more effective. Something like: here are the
six people who need you today, here is why, here is who is most at risk.

If that is right, it reframes the whole thing. The missing piece is not the workers. India has
those. The missing piece is the layer that tells each of them who needs attention today.

## What we are worried about

Measuring a health worker on unresolved items would turn them into a target rather than someone
being supported. Any system that assigns work has to be designed against that from the start, not
retrofitted once it has happened.

## What would count as an answer

- A realistic picture of current workload and capacity, not the establishment figures
- Evidence on what actually helps a frontline worker versus what adds reporting burden
- A defensible approach to prioritisation when there is more need than capacity

## Help us with this

ASHAs, ANMs, Anganwadi workers, supervisors, and anyone who has run community health programmes:
you know things about this that are not written down anywhere. Please tell us.
