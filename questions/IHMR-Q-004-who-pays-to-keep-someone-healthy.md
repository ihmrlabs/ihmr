# Who pays to keep someone healthy?


Insurance mostly monetises catastrophe. It pays when something has already gone wrong. The
question we cannot yet answer is who funds the first unit of improved health, before anything
has gone wrong at all.

## Why it matters

Every part of this depends on someone being willing to pay for maintenance. If nobody is, the
rest is an interesting design exercise.

## Where our thinking currently sits

We suspect financing should attach to a specific need rather than treating "insurance" as one
monolithic thing. Something like:

```text
a health need
    ├── public entitlement
    ├── insurer coverage
    ├── employer contribution
    ├── programme subsidy
    └── individual contribution
```

Everyone who bears the cost of poor health has some reason to fund avoiding it. Government
carries the public health burden and the productivity loss. Insurers pay the claims. Employers
absorb absenteeism. Individuals pay out of pocket, which in India is a great deal.

That is the theory. Whether it survives contact with actual incentives is exactly what we do not
know.

## What would count as an answer

- Real evidence on which preventive interventions are cost-saving, cost-neutral, or simply worth
  paying for on other grounds. Not everything preventive saves money, and pretending otherwise
  would discredit the whole argument
- A view on how blended funding resolves without creating perverse incentives
- Something an insurer would actually underwrite
- Clarity on what government should guarantee versus enable

## Help us with this

Health economists and insurers, particularly anyone who has tried to build a preventive product
in India and found out why it is hard. Negative results are extremely welcome here.
