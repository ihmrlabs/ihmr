# Governance

How IHMR decides things, how positions change, and who is accountable.

This describes the project as it honestly is today: **early, small, and openly maintained by
one person.** We would rather tell you that plainly than imply a structure that does not exist
yet.

---

## Current state

IHMR Labs is maintained by **[Tejas Parthasarathi Sudarshan](https://tejassuds.com)**.

There is no board, no funding body, no institutional sponsor, and no government affiliation.
Decisions are currently made by the maintainer, in public, with reasoning recorded.

**This is a real limitation, and we would rather name it than hide it.** A project thinking
about national health infrastructure should not be governed by one person for long. Broadening
this is an explicit goal, and everything below is designed to be handed over rather than held
on to. If you would like to help shape how that happens, please get in touch.

## How a position becomes an IHMR position

```text
question → research → proposal (RFC) → comment and review → decision
```

Nothing becomes an IHMR position by being written down. It becomes one by being published as a
**decision**, which requires:

- the decision stated plainly
- the evidence it rests on
- the alternatives considered
- the rationale for choosing this one
- the date
- any decision it supersedes

A decision that cannot articulate the alternatives it rejected is not ready.

## How a position changes

**We expect positions to change.** Our own principle is *evidence before doctrine*, and a
project that never changed its mind would mostly be telling you it was not listening.

When a position changes:

1. The old document is marked `superseded`, with the date and what replaced it.
2. **The old document is not deleted or rewritten.** The reasoning stays discoverable.
3. The new document records what it supersedes and why.
4. The change appears in [`updates/`](updates/) and on the project's *changed our mind* page.

Quietly amending a published claim is not permitted. Corrections are made visibly. For health
and policy claims, a silently amended statement is worse than an openly retracted one — it is
uncitable.

## Review

| Claim type | Review required |
|---|---|
| Clinical | Someone clinically qualified, named |
| Health economics | Someone with relevant economics training |
| Legal or regulatory | Appropriately qualified review, and not treated as legal advice |
| Everything else | At minimum, a named human who has verified the sources |

**Every document is signed off by a named human.** Sign-off means: *I have read this, I have
checked its claims against their sources, and if it is wrong that is my error.* See
[AI_USE_POLICY.md](AI_USE_POLICY.md).

Where the project lacks the expertise to review a claim, it says so and holds the claim rather
than publishing it unreviewed.

## Challenging a position

Please do — including a decision we have already published. Nothing here is above being
questioned.

- Comment on the document at [projectihmr.com](https://projectihmr.com)
- Open a [Discussion](https://github.com/ihmrlabs/ihmr/discussions)
- Open an issue or a pull request

If you find a real error we will act on it and credit you. If we disagree with you, you will
get a recorded reason rather than silence — you took the time, and you deserve an answer.

## Conflicts of interest

If you have a material interest in an outcome — commercial, institutional, political, or
financial — please just say so alongside your contribution. **It does not disqualify anything.**
Declared interests are fine and normal; undeclared ones are what damage a project's credibility.

The maintainer's own affiliations and interests will be declared as and when they become
material.

## What the project will not do

- Claim government endorsement, institutional partnership, or funding it does not have
- Publish clinical guidance
- Present a hypothesis as a decision
- Accept funding or partnership that requires suppressing a finding or removing published work
- Publish personal health information

## Institutional form

**Undecided, deliberately.** IHMR Labs might eventually be an open-source project, a
foundation, a non-profit, a standards body, a research institution, a public-private
partnership, a DPI initiative, or something else.

The research and engineering are designed to remain useful across all of those, and to survive
IHMR Labs ceasing to exist. The licensing is chosen so that the work can be forked, adapted, or
taken up by someone else entirely.

Deciding early would bend the research to fit the vehicle. We would rather let the work show us
what it needs to become.
